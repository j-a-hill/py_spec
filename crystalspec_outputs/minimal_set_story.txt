MINIMAL SET: figures, code and story
in-crystallo UV-vis of R37S HGD films, DTNB-labelled vs apo

Written to accompany crystalspec v0.1.0.  Everything below regenerates from
one command:

    cd crystalspec
    PYTHONPATH=src python -m crystalspec.run \
        --config config/experiment.toml --out outputs


================================================================
THE STORY, IN FOUR CLAIMS
================================================================

Claim 1.  X-rays remove the DTNB label from the crystal, and the effect is
          specific to the label.

    At every one of the four transmission settings the DTNB-soaked films lose
    absorbance in the 395-430 nm TNB window while their matched apo controls do
    not.  The five film positions in each arm separate completely by soak state
    at all four settings (exact permutation p = 0.0079, the 5-vs-5 floor over
    252 splits; Cohen's d = -1.63, -2.45, -2.69, -1.56 at 5, 25, 50 and 100 %
    transmission).

    The result rests on the SIGN being consistent within each arm, not on the
    amplitudes being reproducible.  Taking the endpoint TNB-window difference
    over the three positions that survive longest in each arm, the
    position-to-position SD within the DTNB arm (0.0102-0.0288) is comparable to
    the DTNB-minus-apo difference itself (0.0148-0.0394), because the
    crystalline volume fraction in the probed footprint varies between
    positions.  It is the direction of change that is identical across positions
    and opposite between arms.

    Two position counts appear in the outputs and mean different things: the
    permutation test in TABLE 3 uses all five positions per arm on an
    area-under-curve statistic over the common dose range (hence the 0.0079
    floor over 252 splits), while dose-resolved traces and endpoint differences
    hold the contributing set fixed at the three longest-surviving positions, so
    that a position dropping out mid-series cannot create a step in the mean.

    FIGURE 1, panel (d).  TABLE 3.

Claim 2.  The label loss saturates in the first couple of MGy; the protein
          damage it sits on does not.

    Fitting A = A0 + B exp(-D / d1) at fixed diagnostic wavelengths gives, for
    the 412 nm loss, D90 = 5.0, 1.3, 1.6 and 2.4 MGy at 5, 25, 50 and 100 %
    transmission -- against 25 MGy of usable dose at 25 %.  The label-specific
    chemistry is essentially finished while the experiment is still running,
    which is what a finite accessible pool of mixed disulphide being consumed
    looks like.

    The 310 and 328 nm growth behaves the opposite way: D90 there falls at
    73-94 % of the measured dose range in every long condition, i.e. nothing
    has saturated and the fitted d1 (> 6 MGy) is a LOWER BOUND set by where the
    film failed optically, not a property of the chemistry.  For contrast,
    Sutton et al. (2013) report D90 at 400 nm averaging 0.51-0.77 MGy in
    lysozyme, thirty times earlier than our cutoff.

    FIGURE 2, panels (b) and (c).  TABLES 12 and 13.

Claim 3.  A joint diffraction-plus-spectroscopy experiment is feasible, but
          only just, and only at low dose rate.

    The dose needed to detect the label loss above the measured baseline floor
    is 0.25, 0.53 and 0.71 MGy at 25, 50 and 100 % transmission.  Each is
    divided by the room-temperature diffraction lifetime reported at a
    COMPARABLE collection speed (0.257, 0.300, 0.373 MGy respectively) rather
    than by one fixed value -- the inverse dose-rate effect makes the lifetime
    itself depend on how fast the dose is delivered, and a single denominator
    would hide the trade-off.  The resulting ratios are 0.97 at 25 % but 1.76
    and 1.89 at 50 and 100 %.  Only the lowest dose rate is inside the budget on
    a single crystal; averaging four positions brings the ratio to ~0.5 and
    twenty-five to ~0.2.

    Note that TABLE 15 tabulates the same detection doses against six FIXED
    published lifetimes (0.097-0.373 MGy, Owen et al. 2012) to show the spread;
    its ratio columns are therefore not the same quantity as panel (b) and are
    larger for the two fast conditions.

    The practical reading: watching the label leave during a diffraction
    experiment is doable, but it has to be fast and it wants large single
    crystals, not the multi-position film geometry used here.

    FIGURE 3.  TABLES 14 and 15.

Claim 4.  At 100 K the label survives; at room temperature it does not.

    The same TNB-window measure on single cryo-cooled crystals shows no loss
    across four sequential X-ray exposures, while the matched room-temperature
    film endpoint sits at -0.022.  This is the expected temperature dependence
    of specific radiation damage and is the reason the online-spectroscopy
    experiment in Claim 3 is proposed on cryo-cooled crystals.

    The two datasets are NOT directly commensurable -- different sample format
    (single crystals vs multi-position films), different absolute dose, n = 1
    DTNB crystal.  The figure is built to make that visible rather than to hide
    it.

    FIGURE 4.

================================================================
WHAT IS DELIBERATELY NOT IN THE SET
================================================================

Thirteen figures built during the analysis are not regenerated.  They are kept
under crystalspec/superseded/ for the record.  Reasons, so none of this has to
be rediscovered:

  Band-position fitting (former figures 4, S2; tables 5, 8, 9, 10, 11).
     A -6 to -8 nm DTNB-minus-apo shift in the 310 nm growth band was WITHDRAWN.
     It was an artefact of fitting into a truncated, noisy blue flank: noise
     rises 48-fold from the internal-zero window into 285-300 nm, and the
     fitted shift collapses from -8.1 to -1.4 nm as the fit window start moves
     292 -> 304 nm.  The dihedral-angle interpretation built on it goes with it.

  Kinetic rate constants (former figure 5).
     A biphasic loss is a real finding, but the RATES are not determined:
     bootstrapped over film positions k spans [0.02, 14.9] per MGy at 5 %
     transmission, two orders of magnitude.  Figure 2's exponential D90 is the
     defensible summary of the same information.

  Species assignment (former figure 4, table 7).
     The dominant feature is a POSITIVE band at 307-314 nm that grows in BOTH
     arms, 2.8-7.0x larger than the label loss (TABLE 14, growth_to_loss_ratio).  It is protein radiation
     chemistry, not the label, and no species is named for it without a matched
     reference spectrum.  It also sits on 328 nm, which is why the
     protein-S-S-TNB mixed disulphide cannot be isolated: the DTNB-specific
     difference at 328 nm flips sign across transmission
     (+0.0008, -0.0584, +0.0088, +0.0129) and no level's bootstrap excludes
     zero, whereas at 412 nm all four are negative
     (-0.0266, -0.0415, -0.0174, -0.0199) and all four exclude zero.

  Shoulder / isosbestic diagnostics (former figure S4).
     An isosbestic point is ruled out -- the raw zero crossing spans 382-546 nm
     over the series rather than staying put.  A shoulder at 415-455 nm is
     NEITHER demonstrated NOR excluded: the apparent curvature minimum falls at
     445 nm in DTNB, apo and mother liquor alike, against a 454 nm spectrograph
     centre, and a within-arm split null gives p = 0.06-0.14 depending on the
     smoothing window.  Conclusion recorded here; figure not needed.

  Time-axis variant (former figure 10b).
     Retained as a code path (figure_original_idiom(..., xaxis="time")) rather
             than a figure.  Worth knowing: the loss superimposes BETTER on
     elapsed time (spread 1.74-2.73x) than on absorbed dose (2.60-4.49x) across
     a twentyfold dose-rate range, which is the inverse dose-rate signature and
     has direct precedent (Owen et al. 2012).


================================================================
THE CODE
================================================================

crystalspec/
  config/experiment.toml     file -> condition mapping, declared explicitly so
                             the association is auditable; analysis windows
  src/crystalspec/
    io.py          Andor .asc reader -> Acquisition (1600 wavelengths x N frames)
    registry.py    condition table, matched pairs, buffer acquisitions
    dose.py        transmission -> absorbed dose, from RADDOSE-3D
    onset.py       shutter-opening detection (scans seven bands; the shutter was
                   pressed by hand, so dose must be zeroed at the detected onset)
    optical.py     rejects frames where the film has gone optically bad
    resets.py      spectrometer reset detection and stitching
    scatter.py     power-law (lambda^-3) scatter removal
    pipeline.py    read -> resets -> onset -> dark reference -> optical check ->
                   scatter removal.  ORDER MATTERS: the optical check runs
                   BEFORE scatter removal, because the power-law fit would
                   otherwise absorb the broadband offset that signals damage.
    quantify.py    band integration, slopes, permutation tests, bootstraps
    figures.py     all figure functions; only six are wired into run.py
    style.py       palette and sizing.  Warm = DTNB, cool = apo.
    run.py         the one command

Four modules exist because of things that turned out to be wrong in the first
pass and are worth not repeating: onset.py (the shutter is not synchronised),
optical.py (films fail before the acquisition ends), resets.py (the
spectrometer was re-zeroed mid-run), scatter.py (scattering varies severalfold
between positions and dominates the raw spectra).


================================================================
KNOWN LIMITS
================================================================

  * No absolute quantitation.  The crystalline volume fraction in the beam is
    unknown and varies between positions, so no amplitude converts to a
    concentration or an occupancy.  Only relative measures are reported.

  * Absorbed dose is an estimate.  The shutter was operated by hand; the
    detected onset is an UPPER BOUND on the true shutter time.  RADDOSE-3D was
    run for a 50 x 50 um top-hat beam at 5e12 photons/s, while the chapter
    quotes 20 x 20 um at 8e12 -- this discrepancy is unresolved and is flagged
    in the methods draft.

  * Baseline floor ~0.003-0.004 absorbance, measured not assumed: the
    470-500 nm internal zero is not actually zero in DTNB films at the last
    analysed frame (-0.0026 to -0.0039, position SD 0.0013-0.0029).  The
    TNB-window difference (0.0148-0.0394) is 4-10x above it.

  * The deep UV is throughput-dead below ~280 nm in this configuration (150
    l/mm grating blazed at 300 nm, centred 454 nm, plus EVAL film and mother
    liquor), so the disulphide absorption itself was never reached.  This is a
    configuration limit, not a limit of the method.

  * 100 % transmission rests on 2.08 MGy of usable data against 23.34 MGy at
    25 %; treat it as corroborating, not as an independent replicate.
