CAPTIONS — crystalspec figures 1-4 (formal deliverable set)
=============================================================

Figure 1. Absorbance changes in DTNB-soaked and unlabelled (apo) HGD crystals
track a common protein response and one DTNB-specific label response, both
dependent on absorbed dose rather than elapsed time. (A, B) Difference spectra
(relative to the pre-irradiation frame) at five absorbed doses spanning
0-20 MGy, for DTNB-soaked (A) and apo (B) crystals at 25% transmission. Both
arms show the same 300-350 nm growth band, present regardless of label,
increasing monotonically with dose. (C, D) The same two diagnostic
wavelengths (350 nm, dominated by the shared protein response; 412 nm, the
DTNB thionitrobenzoate label) plotted against absorbed dose (bottom axis) and
elapsed time (top axis), mean +/- s.d. over 5 crystal positions. At 350 nm
(C) DTNB-soaked and apo traces rise together, apo slightly ahead throughout;
at 412 nm (D) the two arms diverge from the first measured dose -- apo rises
weakly while DTNB-soaked falls -- consistent with X-ray-driven loss of the
DTNB label superimposed on a growth process common to both arms. Positions
were fixed at the largest subset surviving to the longest dose (see Methods)
to avoid population-composition artefacts as positions drop out; frames
after optical failure (scattering onset) are excluded.

Figure 2. The 412 nm label-loss trace saturates with a well-defined dose
scale (D50/D90) at three of four transmission settings; at the highest
dose rate the same fit is not identified. (A) Difference spectra for
DTNB-soaked and apo crystals at a representative dose, with the two
diagnostic wavelengths (328, 412 nm) marked. (B) The DTNB-minus-apo
difference at six wavelengths spanning the label band and flanking regions,
against absorbed dose (bottom) and elapsed time (top), 25% transmission.
(C) The 412 nm difference trace at all four transmission settings (5, 25,
50, 100%), each with a fitted single-exponential saturation model
(A = A0 + B*exp(-D/d1); dashed) and the dose to reach 50% and 90% of the
fitted excursion (D50, D90). At 5, 25 and 50% transmission the fit is
well-constrained (R^2 0.92-0.99) and D50/D90 scale together as expected for
this model (D50 = D90 x ln2/ln10 = D90 x 0.301, confirmed to within 1% here).
At 100% transmission the measured range covers only 14% of the fit's own
modelled decay (decay_observed_frac); D50 and D90 there are reported as
lower bounds (>1.0, >1.9 MGy) rather than the unconstrained point estimates
the same fit would otherwise return.

Figure 3. The DTNB label survives a dose comparable to a room-temperature
crystal's own diffraction lifetime, with detectability approaching parity
with that lifetime only at the fastest collection speed tested.
(A) DTNB-minus-apo difference at 412 nm (mean over the 408-416 nm label
band) against absorbed dose, all four transmission settings, with a
published room-temperature crystallographic dose limit (0.257 MGy, matched
to the slowest collection speed tested) marked for reference. (B) The dose
at which the label change first clears a fixed detection threshold
(4x10^-3 absorbance units, an internal-zero noise estimate), divided by the
published crystal lifetime at a comparable dose rate (lifetime interpolated
against dose rate per condition, since the inverse dose-rate effect makes
the reference lifetime itself rate-dependent) -- values at or below 1
indicate the label change is detectable within one crystal's usable
lifetime. The grey square series at the 25% point shows the same ratio
recomputed for averaging over 4 and 25 crystals, illustrating the
alternative route to detectability at dose rates where a single crystal is
insufficient.

Figure 4. Room-temperature and cryogenic (100 K) DTNB-soaked crystals show
the same qualitative difference spectrum, but the room-temperature label
loss at a matched X-ray exposure exceeds the cryogenic endpoint by
comparison with the shared protein-growth feature. (A) Difference spectra
(cryogenic, 100 K) at successive X-ray exposures for three apo and one
DTNB-soaked crystal, wavelength range spanning both the shared growth band
(300-350 nm) and the label band (395-430 nm, shaded). (B) The final exposure
from (A), apo and DTNB-soaked overlaid, showing the same qualitative shape
as the room-temperature difference spectrum (Fig. 1A-B). (C) The 395-430 nm
band mean plotted against X-ray exposure number for the cryogenic crystals
of (A), with the corresponding room-temperature endpoint (same soak
protocol, single higher-dose exposure) marked as a dotted reference line in
each arm's own colour. The DTNB-soaked room-temperature endpoint sits
several-fold below every cryogenic DTNB exposure point, while the apo
room-temperature endpoint falls within the spread of the cryogenic apo
series -- consistent with the label response, specifically, being
suppressed at 100 K relative to room temperature.
